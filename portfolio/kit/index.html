The Assistant Kit

Six templates I built from real work with a remote client, to keep expectations clear: what was asked, what is done, what is included and what happens when something goes wrong. Copy them and adapt them. Every example here is invented; nothing comes from a client.

Template 1

One-page weekly report

For clients who are busy and cannot read long documents. One page, honest numbers, one clear next step.

Weekly summary: [project or channel] · Week of [dates]

  1. Key numbers (this week vs. last week): [metric 1], [metric 2], [metric 3], each with its change in %.
  2. Top 3 results this week: [item, number]
  3. What I think is happening: two sentences, with the reason.
  4. Recommendation for next week: one or two actions.
  5. Hours this week: [x h] (by task: [task a, task b])
  6. I need from you: [decision, file or approval] by [date].

Tip: include the bad numbers too. A report that only shows good news stops being read.

Template 2

Publishing procedure, with a final check

My first version of this procedure had seven steps and no final review. This is version 2: step 6 is the one that was missing.

#StepWhat to do
1SelectFilter the log for "not scheduled". Prioritize by the client's goal.
2EditFormat, closing call to action, music, length. Keep one visual style.
3TitlesWrite three options. Send them for approval if the client asked to see them.
4DescriptionFixed call to action, guest links, hashtags. Short paragraphs with blank lines between them.
5Upload and scheduleLink the right full episode as the related video.
6Final checkGo through the checklist below. Nothing goes live without the client's OK.
7LogDate, status and link in the shared sheet.
8MonitorReview CTR and retention. Adjust titles after about three days.

Write down your own time estimate for the whole process (mine was 7.5 to 10 hours for 15 short videos) and log every task in a time tracker. It answers "how long does this take?" before the client asks.

Template 3

Publishing checklist

Five points plus the client's OK, two minutes in total. The most expensive mistakes are the small ones.

  • Names are spelled exactly (guest and client).
  • Right episode, right link, and the closing banner matches the episode.
  • Music volume is comfortable and there is no copyright claim.
  • Maximum resolution; subtitles do not cover faces or images.
  • Title, description (with spaces), tags and preview image reviewed.
  • Client's OK received.

Template 4

Three-line task confirmation

Send it right after each request, in the same channel and format the client used.

Understood: [what you asked for, in my words]

I will: [what, in which format] by [day and time, with the time zone]

I need: [file, decision or access] from you by [day]

Example: Understood: a 40-second reel from the interview, with subtitles and the new title. I will send it Thursday by 6 pm (Buenos Aires time). I need the final cut of the video by Wednesday.

Rule: reply within two hours on working days, even if only to confirm.

Template 5

One-page scope and payment agreement

Agreed at the start. It prevents most disagreements. This is a work template, not legal advice: adapt it to your country.

  1. Parties and start date: [names, date]
  2. Included: [list of deliverables and how many per month]
  3. Not included (quoted separately): [for example design work, shopping research, new channels]
  4. Rounds of corrections: [for example 2 per deliverable]
  5. Rework: rework caused by my own error is not billed. Rework caused by changed instructions is quoted or billed.
  6. Channel: one channel for tasks ([WhatsApp, Telegram or email]) and one format ([audio, text]).
  7. Response times: within [x hours] on working days. Weekend work is agreed by Friday.
  8. Hours and reports: time tracked by task; a one-page weekly report and a detailed monthly report.
  9. Payment: [rate, currency, fees, due date, method]. Paid in advance or in packs of hours.
  10. What success means: [metric and target, with a review date]. Any change to these targets is written down.
  11. Notice to end: [for example 14 days], with an orderly handover of all files.

Prioritized task list

One shared list. The client sets the priority; I set the plan.

Priorities: 🔴 urgent (within 24 hours) · 🟡 important (up to 2 days) · 🟢 can wait (up to 5 working days). Review it together once a week.

Bonus

Questions for the first call

  • How do you prefer to give me tasks: voice notes, text, a shared list?
  • What counts as urgent for you, and which time zone should I plan for?
  • How many rounds of corrections are included, and who approves before anything is published?
  • What does success look like in three months, in numbers, and who decides it?
  • How and when do I invoice, and in which currency?
  • What should I do when the instructions change?
